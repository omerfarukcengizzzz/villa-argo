# ULYSSES MOORE, BOOKS 1–6: MERGED STORY REFERENCE

**Purpose:** reference for a small, private 3D co-op adventure game based on the first six books (the "first cycle").
**Built from:** three independent AI research reports (**Hornet**, **Grok**, **Gemini**), Gemini's own source check of its claims (22 questions), and a limited check of key quotes by Claude. Hornet's report is the base because it was the most careful about evidence.
**Merged:** 27 October 2026. **Second source check:** later the same day, Claude opened the key wiki and review pages directly (see "Second source check" below).

> **What this is not.** Nobody involved read the six novels. Everything here comes from publisher synopses, short original-text previews, encyclopedias, fan wikis, reviews and school book reports. Where those don't establish an answer, this document says **UNKNOWN** instead of filling the gap. Anything you invent for the game should be labeled **adaptation**, not **canon**.

### Confidence labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|

| **HIGH** | A primary source: a publisher synopsis, an original-text preview or a table of contents (from Hornet's sources P1–P12). |

| **LOW** | One report or one fan source only, not checked. |

## 3. THE CORE MYSTERY AND THE RULES

### The mystery
- **Villa Argo** looks haunted: footsteps, a scarred locked door, journals left by its former owner **Ulysses Moore**, who is supposedly dead or gone. HIGH [H]
- **Kilmore Cove** is missing from maps, has no reliable phone or Internet signal, has a railway that leads nowhere, and has a **statue of a king who never reigned**. These are plot mysteries, not mistakes to fix in level design. HIGH [H]
- **Doors to Time** are hidden around the village. Most lead to **one fixed destination** (a "Port of Dreams" / *Porto di Sogno*). The **Villa Argo Door + Metis** is the exception: the ship can reach several destinations. MEDIUM [H][Gk][Gm]
- **The old group:** in the **"Great Summer" of 1958**, Ulysses and his friends discovered the Doors and swore to protect them. They are called the *Viaggiatori Immaginari* (Imaginary Travellers). Members according to the Polish fan wiki: **Ulysses Moore, Peter Dedalus, Leonard Minaxo, Black Vulcano (Victor/Wiktor), Clitennestra "Clio" Biggles, Calypso, Phoenix Smith (Father Phoenix).** MEDIUM for the group / LOW for the exact list [H][Gk][Gm]. **Penelope** is linked to the group but not on that list; **Cleopatra Biggles is not a member** (Gemini retracted this).
- **The villains:** **Oblivia Newton**, a rich and greedy woman, with her henchman **Manfred**, wants the map, then the First Key, to control the Doors and profit from them. HIGH [H][Gk][Gm]. A detailed business plan or a named company is UNKNOWN.

### Rules that are actually established
1. **Doors are physical and locked.** Each has a place and a key. The Argo route has more obstacles after the Door: a stone junction, a dark stair, rotating floor stones and a hatch, before the Metis. HIGH/MEDIUM [H]
2. **Fixed vs. flexible destinations.** Village Doors lead to one place each; Argo/Metis can reach many. MEDIUM [H][Gk][Gm]
3. **The Metis needs a chosen destination.** In B1 the children can't sail it like a normal boat until the captain's journal tells them to pick a destination. Jason shouts **"Egitto!" (Egypt!)** and the helm vibrates. MEDIUM [H][Gm, quoting school report R1]. The ship's exact controls are UNKNOWN. ✖ The idea that it needs "a tribute of stories or memories" is retracted [Gm-orig].
4. **Splitting the party.** In B2 the Door closes after only Julia gets through, and the boys can't follow. Accounts suggest a Door can't be used normally while some travellers are still on the other side. Treat this as **what happened in B2**, not a proven universal law. MEDIUM [H][Gk][Gm]
5. **Doors left open let people through.** In B4, two Venetians come through a Door left ajar into Villa Argo. LOW–MEDIUM [Gm]
6. **Language changes.** Jason could understand Egyptian *in Punt* but not after returning home. The explanation is UNKNOWN; you can show translated speech in Punt scenes, but "automatic translation magic" is an interpretation. HIGH [H, P3]
7. **Villains can travel too.** Being evil doesn't stop anyone from using a Door. HIGH [H]
8. **Time passes in both places at once.** Characters act in Kilmore Cove while others are away (Julia in B2, Rick in B6). MEDIUM [Gm][H]
9. **Dedalus's machines aren't magic.** They are recognizable inventions: printing type, gears, railway controls, a ticket printer with odd keys. HIGH [H, P4, P5]

## 7. BOOK-BY-BOOK PLOT

Scenes are numbered for sequencing, **not** chapter numbers. Each line gives: place → who → what happens → the clue or how it's solved.

### BOOK 1: *La porta del tempo / The Door to Time*

1. **Arrival.** Villa Argo; parents, twins, Nestor. Mrs Covenant notices the scarred four-lock door on the east wall; Nestor says the keys are lost and it leads to a cistern. HIGH
2. **Footsteps.** Stairs and tower; Jason. He hears footsteps; portraits and the mirror door add to the mystery. HIGH
3. **Exploring the house.** The parents leave for London; Rick joins; the three explore the rooms. MEDIUM
4. **Cliff rescue.** Jason slips; Julia and Rick save him. He finds a box with **glowing clay balls** and a **coded parchment**. MEDIUM
5. **First cipher.** Library: they decode the parchment with the **Dictionary**; it points to a dark cave and a ship. MEDIUM
6. **Hidden door.** A pen rolls behind the wardrobe, and they find the four-lock door behind it. MEDIUM
7. **Diary and receipt.** Tower: Jason lifts a ship model resting on a diary, and a **post-office receipt** falls out. MEDIUM
8. **Collecting the keys.** They cycle to the post office and get a parcel with **four animal keys** and a second coded message. MEDIUM
9. **The key puzzle.** Jason tries one key in every lock, which fails. **Rick sees that the initials spell APRI** (in English, **OPEN**). The door opens. MEDIUM
10. **The round room.** Four exits; Jason splits the letters on the floor into words; they pick an exit and find a stair. MEDIUM (why that exit is right: UNKNOWN)
11. **Glowing clay.** The candle goes out; the terra-luce balls light the way. MEDIUM
12. **Floor stones.** **Julia** turns four stones 90° to open a trapdoor, then jumps down first. MEDIUM
13. **The Metis.** A cave beach; the ship; Jason sees that the captain's log is in the same handwriting as the diary. MEDIUM
14. **Departure.** The log says to choose a destination; **Jason shouts "Egypt!"**; the ship sails. MEDIUM

### Mistakes to avoid
1. Revealing Nestor's identity before B6.
2. The children keeping the map at the end of B2 (Oblivia takes it).
3. Making B3 another trip abroad (it's an investigation at home).
4. Treating the Venice "d" clue as a handwritten signature (it's a **printed typeface**, and Alberto printed the book).
5. Treating the valve routine as a known solution (its purpose is unknown).
6. **Giving Black Vulcano the First Key**, or having the children find it with him in the Garden.
7. Putting Rick in the medieval Garden at the **start** of B6 (he's in Kilmore Cove, unmasks Nestor, and only then travels there with Ulysses).
8. Using Atlantis, Agarthi, Thule or El Dorado as B1–6 destinations.
9. **Mixing languages in the key puzzle:** Italian animals with "OPEN", or English animals with "APRI".
10. Presenting anything invented as canon.
